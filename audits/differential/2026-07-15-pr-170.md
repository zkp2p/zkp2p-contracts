## Findings

### Critical

None identified.

### High

#### H-01 — Taker-controlled fees bypass configured deferred chargeback coverage

- **Locations:** `contracts/OrchestratorV3.sol:285-300`, `contracts/RiskManager.sol:404-412`, `contracts/lib/ReferralFeeLib.sol:8-45`
- **Confidence:** High

**Description**

Deferred payout is admitted as the alternative backing source when stake covers the griefing bond but cannot cover the configured chargeback reservation. The approved policy defines post-settlement coverage from the exact released amount, and a 10,000 bps reserve is intended to provide 100% coverage.

OrchestratorV3 instead distributes protocol, referral, and manager fees before invoking `DeferredPayoutHook`, so the hook receives only `netAmount`. Referral fees are selected by the taker and may total 50%. `registerDeferredPayout` calculates coverage from the gross released amount but then silently reduces it to `min(net payout, expected coverage)`.

**Exploit scenario**

1. A chargebackable platform is configured for 10,000 bps coverage and deferred payout.
2. A taker with only enough stake for the griefing bond signals a deferred intent and assigns a 50% referral fee to an address they control.
3. On fulfillment, half of the released on-chain proceeds are paid immediately to the taker's referral address. Only the net remainder reaches StakeVault.
4. The RiskManager accepts roughly 50% coverage despite the configured 100% ratio.
5. If the fiat payment is validly charged back, the LP can recover only the held remainder while the taker retains the immediate self-referral proceeds.

Protocol and manager fees can deepen the shortfall. The same issue exists whenever total fees leave net proceeds below `ceil(releasedAmount * reserveBps / 10_000)`.

**Impact**

The deferred exception can be intentionally undercollateralized below the platform's advertised reserve ratio. At the deployed 100% reserve ratio, taker-controlled referral fees can turn nominal full coverage into approximately half coverage and create a profitable chargeback path.

**Remediation**

Do not silently reduce configured coverage. As the least invasive fail-closed fix, require `_amount >= expectedCoverage`, store `reservedAmount = expectedCoverage`, and revert with an explicit coverage-shortfall error otherwise. This makes deferred settlement with incompatible fees fail rather than misrepresent coverage.

For the complete product behavior, either:

1. route the gross amount into deferred custody and distribute fees only after the coverage window, with chargeback accounting deciding which amounts remain payable; or
2. reserve the fee-created shortfall from stake and include it in admission.

Add an end-to-end case with a 50% self-referral, plus protocol and manager fee combinations around the configured reserve ratio.

### Medium

#### M-01 — Free cancellation after the cliff deterministically underflows and cannot reconcile

- **Location:** `contracts/RiskManager.sol:698-725`
- **Confidence:** High

**Description**

A free position snapshots the platform's nonzero griefing slope and maximum griefing bond but intentionally reserves zero stake. `_cancelPosition` applies the bonded griefing formula to every mode. Once a free intent is cancelled after its cliff, the calculated penalty is positive and line 712 computes `0 reservedAmount - positive penalty`, reverting with arithmetic underflow.

**Exploit / failure scenario**

1. A user consumes a configured free take on a non-chargebackable platform with a nonzero griefing slope.
2. The intent is cancelled or expires after the cliff.
3. The terminal callback reverts. OrchestratorV3 deliberately fail-opens, unlocks LP liquidity, and records the original cancellation timestamp.
4. The RiskManager position remains `PENDING`; every permissionless reconciliation repeats the same underflow forever.

**Impact**

The implementation violates the explicit invariant that free positions reserve and slash no stake and the required terminal lifecycle for free intents. On-chain risk state and indexer state remain permanently stale, and the failed-cancellation record has no successful recovery path. No stake is stolen because none was reserved.

**Remediation**

Handle `RiskMode.FREE` explicitly in `_cancelPosition`: charge zero, release zero, record the terminal cancellation, and never call StakeVault. Preserve an elapsed value only if useful for event observability. Add direct and failed-callback reconciliation tests for free cancellation strictly after the cliff and after the maximum intent period.

#### M-02 — Escrow and stake assets are not bound to the same economic unit

- **Locations:** `contracts/RiskManager.sol:184-191`, `contracts/hooks/DeferredPayoutHook.sol:80-84`
- **Confidence:** High that the invariant is absent; Medium on impact because LPs explicitly opt deposits into the hook.

**Description**

EscrowV2 deposits accept an arbitrary ERC-20, while StakeVault denominates stake, compensation, and deferred liabilities in one immutable USDC-compatible token. RiskManager reads the deposit but never checks `deposit.token == stakeVault.stakeToken()`. Every reservation and penalty therefore treats raw Escrow token units as raw stake-token units.

**Failure scenario**

A non-USDC deposit with different decimals or value is opted into RiskManager. A 100% chargeback reserve may cover only a tiny fraction of the deposit's economic value, or a griefing penalty may demand a disproportionate amount of USDC. Deferred settlement fails later because the canonical hook accepts only its immutable payout token, leaving the intent unfulfillable through that path.

**Impact**

Coverage ratios and free-take amounts lose their stated economic meaning, and takers or LPs can be materially over- or under-protected. The deferred path becomes a late lifecycle denial rather than failing at admission.

**Remediation**

At admission, require `address(deposit.token) == address(stakeVault.stakeToken())` and use a dedicated mismatch error. Also validate in the DeferredPayoutHook constructor that its payout token equals the vault stake token. If the wider protocol guarantees USDC-only deposits through another boundary, codify that boundary on-chain and document it as a RiskManager invariant rather than relying on deployment convention.

### Low / Informational

#### L-01 — An accepted `uint64` risk window can make settlement recovery permanently impossible

- **Locations:** `contracts/RiskManager.sol:524-542`, `contracts/RiskManager.sol:779`, `contracts/RiskManager.sol:987-989`
- **Confidence:** High

**Description**

Platform validation accepts any nonzero `uint64 riskWindow`. A value near `type(uint64).max` is snapshotted successfully, but every real settlement then computes `settledAt + riskWindow`, which exceeds `uint64` and reverts.

For a stake-backed position, OrchestratorV3 fail-opens and completes payout, while the full pending reservation remains locked. Every reconciliation deterministically reverts again. For a deferred position, the mandatory hook also synchronizes settlement and reverts, so fulfillment/manual release cannot complete.

This requires owner misconfiguration rather than an untrusted caller, so it is rated Low despite the persistent lock/DoS impact.

**Remediation**

Keep the approved `uint64` config/deadline/release-time ABI and add a documented, governance-approved `MAX_RISK_WINDOW` (for example, 365 days, safely above the current 30-day policy). Reject larger values in `_validatePlatformConfig` and test the exact maximum, maximum plus one, and actual settlement at the maximum.

This is preferable to:

- **Saturation:** setting the deadline to `type(uint64).max` silently converts a bad configuration into an effectively permanent lock and changes the configured economics.
- **Widening deadline types:** mathematically complete, but it requires a broad ABI change across `RiskPosition`, StakeVault structs, events, functions, package exports, and consumers.
- **Checking only `type(uint64).max - block.timestamp` at admission:** not a complete proof because settlement occurs later and an intent can remain unresolved beyond its nominal period.

A bounded operational window is the least disruptive safe design for realistic chain timestamp horizons. Continue retaining the checked settlement conversion as defense in depth.

## Test Coverage Assessment

The PR has unusually strong line/branch-oriented Hardhat coverage and useful Foundry fuzz/invariant coverage. Verified cases include maximum-of-two reservation math, shared portfolio bounds, policy snapshots, linear/capped penalties, partial chargebacks, exact-deadline rejection, callback reconciliation, partial withdrawals, delegation, controller binding, deferred custody, and solvency.

At the reviewed head, the tests did not cover the adversarial cross-contract compositions that exposed the findings:

- free cancellation after the cliff (the existing free cancellation occurs before a positive penalty accrues);
- deferred payout with taker-controlled referral fees or aggregate fees above the uncovered margin;
- RiskManager admission on a deposit token different from StakeVault's token;
- rejection of an unsafe risk window (the current test only proves settlement later reverts on an artificial overflowing record).

At the reviewed head, the invariant handler also created only stake-backed/free positions and did not exercise deferred payout, chargeback submissions, partial withdrawals during active reservations, failed callbacks, or free cancellation after the cliff. Adding a multi-actor deferred/fee handler and a free-cancellation transition would materially strengthen the security properties beyond reported coverage percentages.

## Notable Verified Invariants

The following properties held under code review for valid same-token configurations and non-overflowing windows:

- A bonded pending reservation is the maximum of the snapshotted maximum griefing bond and chargeback reserve, not their sum.
- Capped elapsed time makes the bonded cancellation penalty no greater than the reserved maximum griefing bond.
- StakeVault shared reservations and partial withdrawal exclusion preserve `reservedStake <= eligibleStake <= stakeBalance` through release and slashing.
- Stake and deferred slashing conserve vault liabilities by moving the same amount from stake/deferred liabilities to LP compensation.
- Position-specific controller snapshots allow old controllers to settle only their own reservations after a delayed controller handover; a new controller cannot seize prior positions.
- Chargeback EIP-712 data binds chain, RiskManager, Orchestrator, intent, platform, amount, evidence id, nonce, and validity interval; manager-wide nonces prevent accepted-attestation replay.
- Chargeback compensation is capped by remaining tracked coverage, and the exact coverage deadline is half-open consistently with beneficiary withdrawal/maturity.
- State effects precede StakeVault calls and all relevant RiskManager entry points are reentrancy guarded; a downstream revert unwinds both contracts atomically.
- Failed cancellation reconciliation uses the durable liquidity-unlock timestamp rather than reconciliation time.

## Remediation Verification

The implementation author addressed every finding in `cd2e78d` (`fix(risk): enforce coverage safety invariants`) and hardened deployment policy in `84bfdfa` (`fix(deploy): require ratified risk policy`). The reviewer recommendations map to the final implementation as follows:

| Finding | Resolution | Verification |
|---------|------------|--------------|
| H-01 | `registerDeferredPayout` now requires the amount actually transferred into deferred custody to cover the exact configured reserve. It reverts with `InsufficientDeferredPayoutCoverage` instead of silently reducing coverage. | End-to-end Hardhat coverage includes a 50% self-referral shortfall and the direct registration boundary. |
| M-01 | Free-mode cancellation bypasses bonded griefing math, records a zero penalty, and performs no StakeVault mutation. | Direct cancellation, Escrow expiry, and failed-callback reconciliation are tested after the cliff. |

The independent product review also identified that illustrative launch parameters could have been applied to a nonlocal network. `84bfdfa` removes those defaults: only local Hardhat networks carry sample policy, and every nonlocal deployment now fails unless its network has explicit governance-ratified configuration.
